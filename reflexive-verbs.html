Spanish Reflexive Verbs — The "Self" Verbs

Jo kaam banda khud par kare — uthna, nahana, kapre pehenna — un verbs ko reflexive kehte hain. Roz ki routine inhi se banti hai.

What "reflexive" means

A reflexive verb describes an action you do to yourself: levantarse = to get (yourself) up, ducharse = to shower (yourself). You can spot one by the -se at the end of the infinitive. When you use it, a small pronoun must travel with the verb:

PersonPronounllamarse (to be called)
yomeme llamo
tútete llamas
él / ellasese llama
nosotrosnosnos llamamos
vosotrososos llamáis
ellossese llaman

The pronoun normally goes before the verb: Me levanto a las siete.

Same verb, two lives

Many verbs exist in both versions — the meaning shifts:

  • levantar = to lift (something else): Levanto la caja. — I lift the box.
  • levantarse = to get up (yourself): Me levanto temprano. — I get up early.
  • llamar = to call (someone): Llamo a mi madre. — I call my mother. (from the app's verb pack)
  • llamarse = to be called: Me llamo Farrakh. — My name is Farrakh.

The pronoun can also attach to the end of an infinitive: Quiero levantarme temprano — I want to get up early.

Common mistakes

  • Dropping the pronoun: ❌ Yo levanto a las siete. → ✅ Yo me levanto a las siete. Without me, you are saying "I lift (something) at seven".
  • Using se for everybody: ❌ Yo se levanto. → ✅ Yo me levanto. Each person has their own pronoun (me/te/se/nos/os/se).
  • Answering "¿Cómo te llamas?" with a bare name: ✅ Me llamo… is the complete, natural answer.
  • Forgetting the stem change: ❌ Me desperto. → ✅ Me despierto.
