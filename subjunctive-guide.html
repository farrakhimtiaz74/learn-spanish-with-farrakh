The Spanish Subjunctive — A Simple Guide

Subjunctive sunkar darne ki zaroorat nahi — ye sirf "chahat, shak aur umeed" wala mood hai. Aahista aahista samjho.

What is it?

Spanish verbs have moods. The indicative states facts: Él habla — he speaks (fact). The subjunctive is used when the action is not a plain fact — when it is wanted, hoped, doubted, recommended or imagined: Quiero que hables — I want you to speak (not a fact yet — a wish).

The classic pattern is: [trigger] + que + [different person] + subjunctive.

When it triggers — remember WEIRDO

TriggerExamples of triggers
W — Wishes & wantsquiero que…, espero que…, ojalá…
E — Emotionsme alegro de que…, es triste que…
I — Impersonal expressionses importante que…, es necesario que…, es mejor que…
R — Recommendationsrecomiendo que…, te aconsejo que…
D — Doubt & denialdudo que…, no creo que…
O — Othersbusco un… que… (something not found yet), cuando… (future time)

How to form it — swap the vowel

Take the yo form of the present tense, drop the -o, and add the "opposite" endings: -ar verbs take e-endings, -er/-ir verbs take a-endings.

Personhablar → hable…comer → coma…vivir → viva…

Stems that change (from the yo form)

  • tener: yo tengo → tenga, tengas, tenga…
  • decir: yo digo → diga… · salir: yo salgo → salga… · hacer: yo hago → haga…
  • ser → sea, seas, sea, seamos, seáis, sean · estar → esté, estés, esté…
  • Spelling care: llegar → llegue (g → gu before e), jugar → juegue.

The golden shortcut

If the same person does both actions, there is no que and no subjunctive — just use the infinitive:
Quiero salir. = I want to leave. (I want + I leave)
Quiero que salgas. = I want you to leave. (person changes → subjunctive)

Common mistakes

  • ❌ Quiero que tú hablas. → ✅ Quiero que tú hables. After a wish-trigger + que, the indicative is wrong.
  • ❌ Creo que él tenga razón. → ✅ Creo que él tiene razón. Plain creo que (I believe) states what you hold as true — indicative. Only no creo que flips to the subjunctive.
  • ❌ Quiero que yo salga. → ✅ Quiero salir. Same person on both sides → infinitive, no que.
  • ❌ Espero que vienes. → ✅ Espero que vengas. Hope is not a fact — subjunctive.
