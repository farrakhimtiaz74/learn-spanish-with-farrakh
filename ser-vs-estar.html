Ser vs Estar — Which "To Be"?

Spanish mein "hona" ke do verbs hain — ser aur estar. Ye guide in ka farq hamesha ke liye saaf kar degi.

The one-line rule

English uses one verb — to be. Spanish splits it in two:

  • SER = what something is — identity, origin, description, time. (Think: the famous DOCTOR rule.)
  • ESTAR = how or where something is right now — location, feelings, changing states. (Think: PLACE.)
Personser (to be)estar (to be)
yosoyestoy
túeresestás
él / ellaesestá
nosotrossomosestamos
vosotrossoisestáis
ellossonestán

When to use SER

SpanishEnglish
Yo soy profesor.I am a teacher. (occupation)
Yo soy de Pakistán.I am from Pakistan. (origin)
El examen es difícil.The exam is difficult. (description)
Ahora es la una.It is one o'clock now. (time is always SER)
La mesa es de madera.The table is made of wood. (material)
La fiesta es en mi casa.The party is (takes place) at my house. (events use SER for where they happen)
María es médica.María is a doctor.
Ellos son cocineros.They are cooks.

When to use ESTAR

SpanishEnglish
Estoy cansado.I am tired. (feeling / state)
El libro está en la mesa.The book is on the table. (location)
Tú estás feliz.You are happy. (feeling)
Él está enfermo.He is ill. (health — a temporary state)
María está en Madrid.María is in Madrid. (location)
La puerta está abierta.The door is open. (current state)
Los niños están en la escuela.The children are at school.
La ventana está cerrada.The window is closed.

Careful: the adjective changes the meaning!

With a handful of adjectives, SER and ESTAR give two different meanings. The app's trainer has 12 of these traps — here are the famous six pairs:

AdjectiveWith SERWith ESTAR
listoMi hermano es listo: siempre saca buenas notas.
He is clever — he always gets good marks.		Yo ya estoy listo para salir.
I am ready to go out.
aburridoEste profesor es muy aburrido.
He is boring (his classes bore people).		El niño está aburrido en casa, sin juguetes.
The boy is bored at home, with no toys.
verdeSu coche es verde, muy bonito.
His car is green (its colour).		El plátano está verde todavía; no lo comas aún.
The banana is still unripe — don't eat it yet.
buenoEl cocinero es muy bueno.
The cook is very good (skilled).		La sopa está muy buena hoy, pruébala.
The soup is very tasty today — try it.
vivoMi tío es muy vivo; siempre tiene ideas nuevas.
My uncle is very sharp — always new ideas.		¡El pez todavía está vivo!
The fish is still alive!
maloEs malo (una persona mala).
He is a bad person (by nature).		La leche está mala; no la bebas.
The milk has gone bad — don't drink it.

One more from the trainer: es atenta = she is a considerate person; está atenta = she is paying attention right now.

Common mistakes

  • ❌ Estoy profesor. → ✅ Soy profesor. Jobs and identity are SER — they are what you are, not how you feel.
  • ❌ El libro es en la mesa. → ✅ El libro está en la mesa. Where a thing is located = ESTAR. (Only events — parties, meetings, exams — use SER for their place.)
  • ❌ Soy cansado. → ✅ Estoy cansado. Tired, happy, ill, worried — feelings and states are ESTAR, because they change.
  • Mixing up the trap adjectives: está listo does not mean "he is clever" — it means "he is ready". Clever is es listo.
