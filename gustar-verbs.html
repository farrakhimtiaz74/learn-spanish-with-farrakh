Gustar and the "Backwards" Verbs

"Mujhe pasand hai" Spanish mein ulta chalta hai — cheez tumhein pasand "karti" hai. Ek dafa structure samajh aaya, hamesha ke liye aasan.

The backwards idea

In English you say I like tea — you are the subject. Spanish builds it the other way round: Me gusta el té literally means "Tea pleases me" — the tea is the subject, and you are the person it pleases. That is why gustar mostly appears in just two shapes: gusta and gustan.

Gusta or gustan? Ask the thing

  • gusta + one thing, or + a verb: Me gusta el té. (I like tea.) · Me gusta leer. (I like reading.)
  • gustan + plural things: Me gustan los libros. (I like books.)

To say who more clearly — because le can mean him, her or you-formal — Spanish adds a + person: A ella le gusta el té (She likes tea), A mí me gusta (emphasis: I like it). For "a lot", add mucho: Me gusta mucho.

Common mistakes

  • ❌ Yo gusto el té. → ✅ Me gusta el té. There is no "yo gusto" in normal speech — the thing does the pleasing.
  • ❌ Me gustan el té. → ✅ Me gusta el té. One thing → gusta; several things → gustan.
  • ❌ Me gusta los libros. → ✅ Me gustan los libros.
  • ❌ Le gusta left unclear in a story about two people → add a él / a ella so listeners know who likes it.
