Spanish Pronunciation Guide

Spanish ki khaas baat: jo likha hai, waisa hi parha jata hai. Chand usool seekh lo — har lafz khud parh loge.

First, the good news

Spanish spelling is almost perfectly phonetic: each letter has (nearly) one sound, and the sound never changes with the word. Learn the letters below and you can read any Spanish word aloud — even one you have never seen.

The five vowels — pure and short

VowelSoundIn the word
a"a" as in fathercasa (house), mañana
e"e" as in metté (tea), queso (cheese)
i"ee" as in machine, but shortsí (yes), aquí (here)
o"o" as in more, but shorthola (hello), ojo (eye)
u"oo" as in flute, but shorttú (you), azul (blue)

Vowels never shrink or change — unlike English, the last vowel of mañana is a full, clear "a".

The letters that trick learners

LetterHow it soundsExamples
hAlways silent — never pronouncedhola = "ola", hambre = "ambre"
jA strong throaty "kh" soundjamón (ham), ojo (eye), Juan
gBefore e/i: like j (throaty). Before a/o/u: hard "g"gente (people) vs gato (cat), agua (water)
ll / yIn most of Spain, like English "y" in yesllave (key), llamo in me llamo, yo
ñ"ny" as in canyon — one single letterniño (child), mañana, español
r / rrr = one quick tap; rr = rolled, trilledpero (but) vs perro (dog) — the famous pair
b / vBoth sound like "b" — v is not English "v"vivir starts and ends with a b-like sound
c / z (Spain)Before e/i, c sounds like "th" in think; z also "th"gracias ≈ "grathias", zapato (shoe). In Latin America both are "s" sounds — both are correct, just regional.

Word stress — three rules cover everything

  1. Word ends in a vowel, n or s → stress the second-to-last syllable: ca-sa, ma-ña-na, ha-blo.
  2. Word ends in any other consonant → stress the last syllable: pa-pel, ciu-dad, ha-blar.
  3. Any exception is written with an accent — the accent always wins: ca-fé, rá-pi-do, co-ra-zón, ja-món.

That is the whole system. If there is an accent mark, stress that syllable; otherwise apply rules 1–2.

The rolled R — honestly

The trilled rr is the one sound most learners need weeks for, and that is normal — Spanish children take years too. Honest practice path: start from a quick "d" flick of the tongue (pero said fast), repeat the pair pero / perro slowly with the recorded audio, and do a few minutes daily rather than one long session. Your single-tap r will be understood everywhere while the trill develops.

Common mistakes

  • Pronouncing the h: ❌ "hola" with an h → ✅ just "ola".
  • Saying j like English: ❌ "jamón" with a soft j → ✅ a throaty "kha-món".
  • Reading mañana as "mah-nah-nah": the ñ is "ny" — "mah-nya-na" (and it means both tomorrow and morning).
  • Ignoring written accents: esta (this) vs está (is) and si (if) vs sí (yes) are different words.
  • Swallowing final vowels — every vowel is pronounced, always.
