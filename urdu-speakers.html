Spanish for Urdu Speakers

Urdu bolne walon ke liye Spanish mushkil nahi — kuch cheezein to tum pehle se jantay ho.

Good news: you start with advantages

  • Gender is not new to you. Urdu nouns are masculine or feminine too (yeh kursī, woh mez…), so Spanish el / la feels familiar from day one. English speakers struggle with this; you won't.
  • Respect levels match. Spanish tú (informal "you") and usted (formal "you") work much like Urdu's tū / tum / aap. You already know when each one is right — that instinct transfers directly.
  • Spanish sounds are honest. Five pure vowels, and words are pronounced the way they are written — much closer to the Urdu-script idea of "likha waisa, parha waisa" than to English spelling.
  • Politeness formulas feel natural. Greeting properly before getting to the point, saying please and thank you generously — Spanish social habits will feel closer to home than you expect.

One honest difference: word order

Urdu is an SOV language: main khānā khātā hū̃ — literally "I food eat". Spanish is SVO: Yo como la comida — "I eat the food", verb before the object, like English. The order feels backwards for the first few days, then the app's sentence drills and dialogues make it automatic. Spanish word order is also fairly flexible, so early mistakes rarely block understanding.

The hard parts — said plainly

  • The rolled R. The strong trill in perro or carro takes practice for almost everyone. The app's recorded clips and Pronunciation Clinic give you the sound to copy, slowly and clearly.
  • Gender endings have rebels. Usually -o is masculine and -a is feminine — but el día and la mano break the rule. In the app, flashcards always carry the article with the word, so you learn la mano as one unit, not a trap.
  • Two verbs for "hona". Urdu manages with one verb; Spanish splits it into ser (what something is) and estar (how or where it is right now). It's the single biggest grammar idea for Urdu speakers — Lesson 7 and the Ser vs Estar guide break it down gently.
  • The subjunctive. Spanish has a whole verb mood for wishes, doubts and "I want you to…" that Urdu doesn't mark the same way. The app only introduces it at B2/C1 (Lessons 41 and 53), in small steps, when you're ready.

How this app is built for you

  • Every word, three ways: English meaning, Roman Urdu meaning, and Urdu script meaning — learn in the script you think in.
  • Grammar explained in Roman Urdu inside every lesson, not in heavy English textbook language.
  • A real recorded voice: 7,902 clips of Spain Spanish (es-ES). Tap any word, sentence or dialogue line and hear it properly.
  • Speaking from day one: listen-and-repeat practice, speaking exams and a Conversation Simulator — with the mic only when you tap it.
  • Urdu-script beta: an optional setting switches the app's dock and main headings into Urdu script (the rest stays Roman Urdu — it's labelled beta, honestly).
  • Made for real life: fully offline, no ads, PIN lock, two profiles on one phone — and a real-life 30-day plan to follow.
